What reglazing actually involves

If you have never had a surface refinished, here is what happens in your bathroom on the day.

Preparation and repair

The room is ventilated and every surface that is not being coated gets masked: floor, walls, fixtures, mirrors and vents. Old caulk comes out. Rust, chips, cracks and drill holes are ground back, filled and sanded flat. The porcelain or fiberglass is then cleaned with a degreaser and etched so the new coating has something to grip. On tile reglazing jobs, grout lines are cleaned and repaired at this stage as well, which is why a refinished tile wall looks uniform rather than patchy.

Coating and cure

A bonding primer goes on first, followed by several thin coats of the finish in your chosen color. Thin coats are what create a hard, even gloss. Heavy coats sag and stay soft. The surface is then left to cure, and we tell you exactly how long to wait before using it, usually around 24 hours for a bathtub. Before we leave, masking comes down, the room is cleaned, and you get written care instructions covering which cleaners to use and which to avoid.

Frequently asked questions

The questions we hear most often about bathtub reglazing, tile reglazing and refinishing in general.

What is bathtub and tile reglazing, and how does it work?

Reglazing is a surface restoration process. The existing bathtub or tile is deep cleaned, repaired and chemically etched, then coated with a bonding primer and several thin layers of a durable finish. The result is a hard, glossy surface that looks new while the original fixture stays exactly where it is. Nothing is torn out and no plumbing work is needed.

How long does a reglazing job take?

Most bathtub reglazing and shower reglazing projects are finished in two to four hours. Larger jobs such as a full bathroom tile reglazing or a multi unit commercial tile reglazing schedule take longer, and we give you a time estimate with your quote. Replacement, by comparison, usually takes several days once demolition, plumbing and tile repair are included.

How long will a reglazed surface last?

With non abrasive cleaners and normal use, a professionally reglazed bathtub or tile surface lasts many years. The two things that shorten its life are scouring powders and leaving standing water on the finish from a dripping faucet. We cover both in the care instructions we leave with you.

How much does bathtub refinishing cost in New York?

Affordable bathtub refinishing typically starts around $350, compared with roughly $3,000 for a replacement once labor, tile repair, plumbing and disposal are counted. The final price depends on the size of the surface, the amount of chip and crack repair required, and whether you are reglazing a tub alone or a tub and surrounding tile together. Estimates are free.

Can you reglaze while a home or building is occupied?

Yes. Occupied homes, apartments, hotels and nursing homes are most of our work. We ventilate the room, contain the area and use processes that keep odor and mess to a minimum. In a building with several bathrooms, residents can usually go about their day normally while one room is out of use.

Can I choose a color other than white?

Yes. White and off white are the most requested finishes, but we can match an existing color or move you to a different one. Color matching is common on tile reglazing and countertop refinishing projects where only part of a room is being refinished, and on vanity resurfacing where the top needs to match new cabinetry.

Do you repair chips and cracks before reglazing?

Tub chip repair is part of standard preparation. Chips, hairline cracks, rust spots and old drill holes are ground out, filled, sanded flat and then coated along with the rest of the surface, so the repair disappears. We can also carry out chip repair on its own if the rest of the finish is still in good condition.

Which areas do you serve?

We are based in Ridgewood, New York, and cover Queens, Brooklyn, Manhattan, the Bronx, Staten Island, Westchester and Long Island for both residential bathtub reglazing and commercial tile reglazing contracts.

Bathtub Reglazing

A worn bathtub rarely needs to leave the building. Bathtub reglazing strips the tub back to a clean, sound surface, repairs the damage in it and rebuilds the finish with a bonding primer and several thin coats of a hard, glossy coating. We work on cast iron, pressed steel, acrylic and fiberglass tubs, including clawfoot models that would be difficult and expensive to replace. Stains that no longer respond to cleaning, a dull chalky bottom, spreading chips and rust around the drain all disappear. The job usually takes two to four hours, leaves no debris and requires no plumber, which matters in older New York buildings where opening a tiled wall turns a small project into a large one. Tub reglazing also lets you change color, so a dated almond or bone tub can come back a clean modern white. We finish by removing all masking and leaving written care instructions. Estimates are free, and if we find that a tub is cracked through or unstable underneath, we will tell you plainly that replacement is the better call rather than sell you a coating that will not hold.

Tile Reglazing

Bathroom tile reglazing solves two problems at once: tile that has gone dull, stained or hopelessly dated, and grout that has darkened beyond cleaning. We repair cracked and loose tile, clean and restore the grout lines, then etch and coat the whole wall so the finish is even rather than patchy. Pink, blue, green and brown tile from earlier decades is the most common request, and a single day of tile reglazing can change the entire character of a bathroom without touching the wall behind it. Because nothing is demolished, there is no dust in the hallway, no debris to carry down and no risk of disturbing old plaster or pipework. The coated surface is sealed and easy to wipe clean, which is why landlords and building managers use tile reglazing between tenants. Floor tile and kitchen backsplashes can be refinished using the same process. Estimates are free, and we are happy to advise on whether reglazing the tile alone will be enough or whether doing the tub at the same time gives a better result for the money.

Shower Reglazing and Shower Stall Refinishing

Shower stalls take more abuse than almost any surface in a home. Fiberglass units go yellow and rough, pans crack under repeated use, and tiled surrounds lose their seal along the grout lines. Shower reglazing addresses all of it. We repair cracks and soft spots in the base, remove old caulk and mildew, restore grout where needed, then coat the pan and surround in a durable finish that resists water and cleaning products. A slip resistant texture can be added to the floor of the stall, which is a common request in family homes, rental units and assisted living facilities. The result is a stall that is sealed, bright and simple to clean, delivered in hours rather than the days a replacement enclosure would take. For properties with several identical stalls, we can schedule the work unit by unit so rooms stay available. Estimates are free, and we will confirm cure times up front so you know exactly when the stall can go back into service for family, guests or tenants.

We refinish porcelain, enamel over cast iron and steel sinks in both bathrooms and kitchens, including pedestal sinks and integrated basins where replacement would mean new plumbing and a new countertop. Because a sink is a small surface, it is often the most affordable job we do and can be scheduled alongside bathtub or tile work in the same visit. Tub Chip Repair

A dropped shampoo bottle or a shower door installation can take a chip out of a tub in a second, and the exposed metal underneath begins to rust soon after. Tub chip repair stops that spread. We grind out the damaged area, treat any rust, fill and build the surface back level, then sand and blend the repair into the surrounding finish so it is not obvious where the damage was. The same process handles hairline cracks, old drill holes from a removed grab bar, and rust around the drain. If the rest of the tub is still in good condition, a spot repair is far cheaper than reglazing the whole surface, and it is often all that a security deposit inspection or a move out walkthrough requires. If the damage is widespread, we will tell you honestly that full bathtub reglazing is the better value. Estimates are free, and small repairs can often be scheduled alongside another job in the same building so you are not paying a separate call out.
